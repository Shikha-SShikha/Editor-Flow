N-Alkylation of Amides with Benzylic Alcohols Catalyzed by a Cationic Ruthenium(II) Complex

Tapas Mahata and Manish Bhattacharjee*

Department of Chemistry, Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur 721 302, India

Abstract

A ruthenium(II) compound, [(η6-p-cymene)Ru(bpm)Cl]+[PF6]− (1) {bpm = bis(3,5-dimethylpyrazolyl)methane} has been synthesized and structurally characterized. The compound crystallizes in monoclinic space group P21. The compound catalyzes N-alkylation of aromatic amides with benzylic alcohols in the presence of Cs2CO3 in toluene at 120 °C with very good to excellent conversions.

Introduction

Traditionally, the synthesis of amides involves the condensation of carboxylic acids or their derivatives (such as acid chlorides, anhydrides, and esters) with amines.10 In addition, aryl and alkenyl halides were also employed as alkylating agents for the N-alkylation of amide,11 which often necessitated the use of stoichiometric amounts of base, generated halide by-products, and required elevated temperatures, thus presenting environmental and practical challenges. To overcome these limitations, the use of alcohols as alkylating agents for N-alkylation of amines and amides has emerged as a more sustainable alternative.12 […] However, the hydroxyl group of alcohols is a poor leaving group. Thus, the use of alcohols as alkylating agents is challenging and requires the use of transition-metal catalysts.13

Short summary

AI-written

This study reports the development of a cationic ruthenium(II) complex, [(η6-p-cymene)Ru(bpm)Cl]+[PF6]−, as a catalyst for the N-alkylation of amides with benzylic alcohols under mild conditions. The method employs a borrowing hydrogen mechanism using Cs₂CO₃ as a base in toluene and achieves good to excellent yields for a broad range of substrates, including pharmaceutically relevant amides. The catalyst outperforms other ruthenium complexes, and the methodology was successfully applied to the synthesis of the antiemetic drug Tigan.
